# Coverage analysis — how outlets told *Hall v. Fleming*

Companion to `RESEARCH.md`, which holds the primary-source record. Everything here is attributed
to a named outlet. Items marked **unverified** were seen in search results but not retrieved, and
are not quoted or relied on.

## The single most important thing to get right: there are two cases

Two separate lawsuits challenge the same Virginia exclusion, against the same defendant, brought
by Liberty University students, both lost below, both in the Fourth Circuit:

| | *Hall v. Fleming* | *Johnson v. Fleming* |
|---|---|---|
| Plaintiff(s) | Bethany M. Hall | Cameron Johnson, Luke Thomas, Trace Stevens |
| Counsel | **The National Legal Foundation** + Claybrook LLC | **Alliance Defending Freedom** |
| District | No. 3:25-cv-00186-DJN (Novak, J.) | No. 3:25-cv-407 (Young, J.) |
| Fourth Circuit | **No. 25-1574 — decided 13 May 2026** | No. 26-1437 — on appeal |
| Programs attacked | VTAG | VTAG **and** Va. National Guard tuition assistance |
| Produced the published opinion + Richardson concurrence | **Yes** | No |

A large share of the material that surfaces when searching this topic is about *Johnson*, driven
by ADF's press operation. *Hall* is the case that actually made law, and it drew markedly thinner
advocacy coverage — because ADF, whose press shop dominates this space, isn't Hall's counsel.

Misattributions to watch for: the ADF press release, The Christian Post, The College Fix,
Stars and Stripes, and Virginia Lawyers Weekly are all *Johnson* coverage, not *Hall*.

## The finding: the *Locke*-shaped hole

The sharpest divergence isn't ideological. It tracks **who sourced the story**.

Coverage written from ADF's press materials frames the issue as equal treatment and
anti-discrimination — "second-class citizens," students denied grants for choosing the
"wrong" religious degrees — and **does not mention *Locke v. Davey* at all**. The Christian Post
piece and ADF's own case explainer both omit it.

*Locke* is the entire reason both cases lost. Omitting it converts a story about a binding
precedent into a story about official hostility to religion.

Every independently-reported piece — WORLD, Courthouse News, Religion Clause, Volokh's Short
Circuit, Liberty Magazine — puts *Locke* at the center.

## The second finding: the concurrence is the story

The strongest anti-*Locke* rhetoric in the whole record comes from a judge who **voted to
affirm**. Outlets split on whether that's the story or a footnote:

- **Led with it:** Courthouse News; Volokh's Short Circuit; Religion Clause; Law.com/NLJ, whose
  headline is entirely about it — "4th Circuit Judge Urges Supreme Court to Overturn Theology
  Scholarship Ban Precedent" *(unverified — 403 on fetch; headline and date only)*.
- **Omitted it entirely:** Bloomberg Law and WORLD, in the portions that could be read.

WORLD omitting it is the surprise: it's the most sympathetic-to-Hall outlet, and the concurrence
is the single most useful passage for her side.

### The same fact, two headlines

- Courthouse News: "Fourth Circuit upholds Virginia policy **barring tax dollars for pastoral
  degrees**" — the state wins, and the subject is public money.
- Bloomberg Law: "Liberty University Ministry Student **Denied Chance at State Grant**" — a
  student loses, and the subject is her.

Same court, same day, same holding.

## How much of a person Hall is allowed to be

- **WORLD** and **Courthouse News** are the only outlets that make her a person. WORLD reports she
  is a pastor's daughter who feels called to serve as a worship leader, picked up multiple jobs,
  and is out roughly **$20,000**. She gave WORLD an on-the-record interview.
- **Religion Clause**, **Liberty Magazine** and **Volokh** identify her only structurally.
- The gap matters: sympathy in this story is carried almost entirely by biography, and half the
  coverage declines to supply any.

Quotes she gave WORLD (Liz Lykins, 19 May 2026):

> "It's just quite simply wrong and very sad that our Supreme Court made that decision back
> then… whether you agree with Christianity or a different religion or not, it's not up to the
> court to determine if I get to receive a scholarship paid for by taxpayer dollars."

> "That's $20,000 that I just lost because I'm studying what I feel like I should do with my life."

> "If those after me receive this scholarship, that's a win in my book."

## An apparent contradiction that resolves

The opinion (13 May 2026) says Hall "recently completed her sophomore year." WORLD (19 May 2026)
says she "just completed her junior year." Both are right — the opinion describes the record as
it stood when the case was pleaded in early 2025; WORLD describes her a year later. A reader
comparing the two without the dates would reasonably think one got it wrong.

## Errors found in the coverage

In Courthouse News' decision story (13 May 2026):

1. Hall is referred to as "**He**" in one paragraph. She is a woman throughout the opinion.
2. "U.S. Circuit Judge Steven Agee… **concurred** with the other judges." Agee wrote nothing —
   he **joined** Benjamin's majority. Richardson wrote the concurrence. Conflating "joined" with
   "concurred" inverts who said what in the one case where that distinction is the whole story.

Bloomberg Law's accessible summary renders the court as calling *Locke* "perfectly analogous."
The opinion says "**directly** analogous." Flagged as unconfirmed — the raw article is paywalled.

## What nobody wrote

No coverage could be found from the church-state-separation side: **Americans United** and the
**Freedom From Religion Foundation** appear to have published nothing, despite this being a rare
**win** for the exclusion they defend. Also found nothing at Reuters Legal, Law360, ABA Journal,
Religion News Service, SCOTUSblog, Inside Higher Ed, or Higher Ed Dive.

The result: the framing space around this case is occupied almost entirely by one side. A reader
searching it will find advocacy from the religious-liberty side, neutral digests, and — apart
from one Courthouse News headline — essentially no one arguing that Virginia was right.

Nothing from Becket, First Liberty, National Review, The Federalist, Washington Examiner,
The Daily Signal, Baptist Press, Christianity Today, Deseret News, or Liberty University's own
newsroom on *Hall* either.

## Supreme Court status — stated intent, not a filed fact

Steven Fitschen told Courthouse News on 13 May 2026: "The historical analysis in *Locke* was
flawed… We certainly hope that the Supreme Court will grant cert in this case." WORLD reports
Hall intends to pursue it.

**No docketed cert petition could be verified.** Treat a Supreme Court appeal as stated intention
only.
